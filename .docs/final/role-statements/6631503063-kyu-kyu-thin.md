# Personal Role Statement — QA/Test

**Kyu Kyu Thin — 6631503063**

As a QA/Test member of Team Apex, my main responsibility was to ensure that
What2Cook worked correctly and provided a good user experience. I focused
on testing the application, identifying defects, and verifying that the
system met its requirements.

I prepared the Test Plan, which included 15 test cases and 5 User
Acceptance Testing (UAT) tasks, each with clear and measurable pass or fail
criteria. I also developed two functional requirements: F6 (Dietary
Filters) and F7 (Search by Ingredient). In addition, I conducted UAT
sessions with real users, including U07, U08, U09, and U10. I recorded task
completion times, errors, and their severity to evaluate the application's
performance and usability. I also used the System Usability Scale (SUS)
questionnaire, where the application achieved a score of 71.3, above the
average benchmark of 68. Through testing, I identified 17 defects, including
slow AI recipe generation, incorrect leftover quantities, and receipt
scanning failures. I worked with the team to ensure these issues were fixed
and verified through the second round of testing.

This experience improved my attention to detail, problem-solving, and
teamwork skills. Most importantly, I learned that effective quality
assurance requires clear criteria, reliable evidence, and continuous
improvement.

## Reflection

**1. Lessons learned.** Building What2Cook with AI taught me that AI can make
software development faster, but it does not always make the software better.
One thing that got better was development speed. AI helped us generate ideas,
draft specifications, and support recipe generation, which saved the team time.
One thing that got worse was reliability. AI sometimes produced incorrect or
unexpected results, and we found real problems with receipt scanning and AI
recipe generation. As the QA member, I learned that we cannot simply trust AI
outputs. We need clear requirements, proper testing, and human verification.
AI also changed our teamwork: we had to communicate more about AI-generated
results and check together whether they met our requirements. Overall, AI is a
useful assistant, but software engineers remain responsible for quality,
security, and the final product.

**2. Where I stand in the AI era.** AI is changing the software industry very
quickly. I don't think it will completely replace software engineers, but it
will change how we work. I see AI as a tool that improves productivity, not
something that replaces our knowledge and skills. As a Software Engineering
student, I still believe that programming, problem-solving, system design, and
testing are very important. My experience on What2Cook showed me that even when
we use AI, people still need to check the results and make sure the system
works correctly.

**3. My plan.** Next year, I plan to gain practical experience through an
internship and improve my technical skills and problem-solving. I also want to
learn how to use AI tools effectively in software development. In five years, I
hope to be a professional software engineer with strong technical knowledge and
real-world experience, working on reliable systems, designing backend
architectures, and building useful applications.
