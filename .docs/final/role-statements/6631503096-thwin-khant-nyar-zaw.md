# Personal Role Statement — Designer

**Thwin Khant Nyar Zaw — 6631503096**

As the UI/UX designer for What2Cook, I owned the interface from wireframes to
the design system used by developers. I began with low-fidelity wireframes
to define each screen's purpose, structure, and content. Every element
addressed a documented user pain point or a scoped feature, rather than
serving as decoration. I then mapped the core journey: scanning a receipt,
building a pantry, generating recipes, cooking and confirming leftovers, and
creating a grocery list. Each step centered on one primary action to keep
the experience intuitive.

Next, I translated the prototype into a consistent visual language using
semantic colour tokens, a typography scale, and reusable components. This
allowed updates, such as contrast adjustments or theme changes, to be
applied consistently without editing individual screens. Six design
principles guided my decisions, including making the pantry the home screen
and maintaining transparency by showing quantities, selected ingredients,
and low-confidence receipt entries. This helped users understand what the
system had processed or changed.

I also treated compliance as part of the user experience. Consent became a
plain-language choice, while allergen conflicts appeared through a
three-level warning hierarchy. Rather than silently hiding recipes, the
design highlighted risks and left the final decision to the cook.

## Reflection

**1. Lessons learned.** Building What2Cook with AI changed how I understand software engineering. I learned that AI can accelerate development, from turning requirements into prototypes to generating code and documentation. One thing that improved was productivity, especially during prototyping and exploring solutions that would otherwise take much longer. However, one thing that became worse was my tendency to rely on AI when facing unfamiliar problems. Sometimes, generated solutions looked correct but required additional review, debugging, and adjustments. This taught me that AI cannot replace understanding requirements, testing, making design decisions, or considering quality and security. Teamwork also requires clear communication and shared understanding, rather than relying on AI-generated explanations alone.

**2. Where I stand.** I believe I am currently at the beginning of my journey in the software industry. AI has made software development more accessible and changed how engineers work, but it has also raised expectations. Knowing how to use AI is no longer enough; engineers must understand the fundamentals, evaluate AI outputs critically, solve problems independently, and take responsibility for the software they build. I see AI as a tool that strengthens engineering ability when used with proper understanding, not as a replacement for it.

**3. My plan.** Over the next year, I plan to strengthen my foundations in programming, mathematics, machine learning, data engineering, and software development while building practical projects and gaining industry experience. In the next five years, my goal is to become a Senior Machine Learning Engineer who can design, develop, evaluate, and deploy reliable machine learning systems. I want to grow beyond simply using existing AI tools and become someone capable of building meaningful AI solutions, understanding their limitations, and contributing to real-world problems.
