# What2Cook — Research & Findings

This document presents the research findings that support the three main problems identified during the development of What2Cook.

## P1 — Forgotten Leftovers Go Bad

People often forget about cooked meals and leftover ingredients stored in their refrigerators. Without proper tracking, food may remain unused until it spoils and eventually gets thrown away.

### Research 1: Household Food Waste in Thailand (2025)

**Nationwide Household Food Waste Generation Rates in Rural and Urban Settings: Evidence in Thailand**

- Authors: Krutprong & Pharino
- Published in: *Applied Environmental Research*, 47(2), May 2025.
- Sample: 2,700 Thai households monitored over 28 days.
- Findings:
  - 0.332 kg of food waste per person per day, including liquids.
  - 0.228 kg per person per day, excluding liquids.

**Source:** https://ph01.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/aer/article/view/259655

### Research 2: UNEP Food Waste Index Report 2024

- Publisher: United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP).
- Findings:
  - Approximately 1.05 billion tonnes of food were wasted globally in 2022.
  - Households accounted for approximately 60% of global food waste.
  - Thailand's estimated household food waste was 86 kg per person per year.

**Source:** https://wedocs.unep.org/items/dbe2cd4c-8384-4636-8359-5847f42b9711

### Research 3: WRAP — Household Food and Drink Waste in the UK 2021/22

- Publisher: Waste and Resources Action Programme (WRAP).
- Findings:
  - UK households generated approximately 6.4 million tonnes of food waste, valued at £17 billion.
  - Approximately 40% of household food waste was discarded because it was not used in time.

**Source:** https://www.wrap.ngo/resources/report/household-food-and-drink-waste-uk-2022

## P2 — Decision Effort Pushes People Toward Takeout

Deciding what to cook every day can become repetitive and stressful. People may spend considerable time thinking about meal options, and the difficulty of making decisions can make ordering takeout a more convenient alternative.

### Research 1: Australia's Cooking Landscape (2017)

**McCrindle / HelloFresh**

- Sample: 1,005 grocery shoppers.
- Findings:
  - 62% found deciding what to cook at least slightly stressful.
  - Only 20% reported having set meals.

**Source:** https://mccrindle.com.au/app/uploads/images/HelloFresh_Australia27s-Cooking-Landscape_Report_FINAL_1Feb2017.pdf

### Research 2: Dolmio Consumer Survey (UK)

- Sample: 2,000 UK adults.
- Findings:
  - Respondents spent approximately 43 minutes per week deciding what to eat.
  - This represents approximately 37 hours per year.
  - 57% identified deciding what to have for dinner as the most difficult meal-related decision.

**Source:** https://uk.news.yahoo.com/indecisive-brits-spend-37-hours-125045946.html

## P3 — Shopping Blind Leads to Overbuying

People do not always remember which ingredients they already have at home before going grocery shopping. This can result in duplicate purchases, unnecessary spending, and forgotten ingredients that eventually go to waste.

### Research 1: Tesco / YouGov Consumer Survey (November 2022)

- Research associated with: Tesco and YouGov.
- Findings:
  - 92% do not always know what they already have in their kitchen before shopping.
  - 68% have purchased food they already had at home.
  - 73% have thrown away forgotten items from their cupboards, refrigerators, or freezers.
  - 62% have overlooked items because they thought they already had them.
  - Among those respondents, 53% had to change their meal plans as a result.

**Source:** https://www.circularonline.co.uk/news/new-survey-shows-92-of-shoppers-dont-always-know-whats-in-their-kitchen/
